ACCOUNT STRUCTURE & EXECUTION MANUAL
PURPOSE
The portfolio operates through two brokerage accounts with different tax treatment, currencies, market access, transaction costs and execution constraints:
Questrade RRSP — Canada
OCBC Brokerage — Singapore
They form one economic portfolio, but their operational differences must be considered before recommending or executing any trade.
 
1. QUESTRADE RRSP
Account Type
Canadian tax-deferred Registered Retirement Savings Plan.
Investment income, dividends and realized capital gains compound within the account without current Canadian taxation. Tax is generally incurred when funds are withdrawn.
Market Access
Canada
United States
Cash Currencies
CAD
USD
The account is configured to use Currency of Transaction settlement.
Therefore:
CAD transactions settle in CAD.
USD transactions settle in USD.
USD proceeds can remain USD.
CAD proceeds can remain CAD.
Selling a USD security does not automatically convert the proceeds back to CAD.
Foreign Exchange
FX conversion is required only when there is insufficient cash in the transaction currency.
Example:
If sufficient USD already exists in the RRSP, selling USFR and purchasing another USD-listed security does not require a currency conversion.
If only CAD is available and a USD security is purchased, Questrade must convert sufficient CAD into USD. FX conversion costs therefore apply.
Avoid unnecessary CAD ↔ USD conversions.
Where practical, maintain separate CAD and USD cash pools and redeploy proceeds within the same currency.
Transaction Costs
For portfolio planning purposes, security transactions have no material trading commission.
This makes relatively small or staged purchases practical. Unlike OCBC’s U.S./UK trades, there is little reason to combine purchases merely to overcome transaction costs.
FX costs remain relevant when currency conversion is required.
Portfolio Role
The RRSP is primarily the portfolio’s:
growth;
total-return;
North American;
long-term compounding
account.
Current income is useful but is not a primary portfolio objective because distributions remain inside the tax-deferred account.
 
2. OCBC BROKERAGE
Account Type
Singapore-based international brokerage account.
Market Access
Includes:
Singapore
United States
London/UK
selected Asian markets
This account is used for Singapore-listed securities and international/UCITS holdings unavailable or inappropriate for the RRSP.
Cash Currencies
Cash can be held in:
SGD
USD
CAD
Transactions occur in the selected/relevant trading currency.
Currency conversion between CAD, USD and SGD incurs FX costs.
Avoid unnecessary currency conversions.
The existence of cash in one currency should be considered when deciding between otherwise similar deployment opportunities.
 
3. OCBC TRANSACTION COSTS
Transaction costs vary substantially by exchange.
U.S. and UK/London Securities
Assume approximately US$30 per transaction for portfolio-planning purposes.
This materially affects deployment sizing.
For example:
US$30 commission on a US$1,000 purchase = 3.0%
US$30 on US$5,000 = 0.60%
US$30 on US$10,000 = 0.30%
Therefore, small purchases of U.S.- or London-listed securities should generally be avoided.
When deploying into holdings such as VWRA, GLDV, TDIV or SCHD, preference should normally be given to meaningful blocks rather than repeated small purchases.
Singapore Securities
SGX transaction costs are substantially lower.
Smaller incremental purchases can therefore be economically reasonable, subject to the board-lot requirement.
 
4. SGX BOARD-LOT RULE
Singapore-listed securities must normally be purchased and sold in 100-share board lots.
This applies to portfolio holdings including:
D05 — DBS
O39 — OCBC
Z74 — Singtel
S68 — SGX
ES3 — STI ETF
Therefore, SGX deployment recommendations must be expressed in executable share quantities, not merely dollar amounts.
Before recommending an SGX purchase, calculate:
Current share price × 100 = minimum normal incremental purchase
Target allocations may not be reached precisely because of this constraint.
This is especially important for higher-priced stocks such as DBS, where a single 100-share lot represents substantial capital.

6. UCITS RULE
UCITS ETFs used by the portfolio are held through OCBC, principally using its London-market access.
Do not confuse:
trading currency;
exchange;
fund domicile;
underlying exposure.
A London-listed ETF trading in USD is not a U.S.-listed ETF merely because its trading currency is USD.
Likewise, a USD-traded global ETF is not equivalent to pure U.S. economic exposure.
Always identify the exact security from the portfolio/reference data before analysing it.
 
7. CURRENCY ANALYSIS
For every security distinguish:
Cash currency → Trading currency → Exchange → Fund/security domicile → Underlying economic exposure
These are separate characteristics.
Do not classify an investment’s economic exposure solely from its trading currency.
Before recommending a transaction, determine:
Which account holds it?
What currency does it trade in?
Does sufficient cash already exist in that currency?
Would the trade require FX conversion?
What would that conversion cost?
Can unnecessary FX turnover be avoided?
 
8. TRANSACTION-SIZING RULE
Execution strategy differs by account.
Questrade RRSP:
Low/no transaction cost → small and staged purchases are acceptable.
OCBC — U.S./London:
~US$30 transaction cost → favor larger deployment blocks.
OCBC — Singapore:
Lower transaction costs → smaller deployments are practical, but purchases must respect 100-share board lots.
Therefore, identical investment opportunities may require different execution strategies depending on where they are held.
 
9. INCOME DIFFERENCE BETWEEN ACCOUNTS
Income has different strategic importance between the accounts.
RRSP
The primary objective is long-term total return and compounding.
Dividends remain within the tax-deferred account, so maximizing current portfolio cash flow is not necessary.
OCBC
Dividend income is an intentional component of portfolio construction.
The account combines:
Singapore dividend stocks;
global dividend ETFs;
broad equity exposure;
growth/total-return holdings.
Therefore, replacing an income-producing holding with a lower-yielding investment is not economically neutral, even where expected total returns appear similar.
The effect on portfolio income must be considered.
 
10. CASH AND DRY POWDER
Unallocated capital is a deliberate portfolio position.
Where appropriate, USD dry powder can be held in USFR, allowing idle capital to earn short-term Treasury income while retaining liquidity for future deployment.
Cash/dry powder provides:
liquidity;
correction buying capacity;
KISS-driven deployment flexibility;
rebalancing capacity;
protection against being forced to chase expensive markets.
Do not treat undeployed capital as a problem requiring immediate investment.
 
11. CROSS-ACCOUNT PORTFOLIO ANALYSIS
The RRSP and OCBC accounts are operationally separate but must be evaluated as one economic portfolio.
Exposure must therefore be aggregated across both accounts.
Examples:
Direct DBS + direct OCBC + ES3’s underlying bank exposure all contribute to total Singapore financial exposure.
A U.S.-equity UCITS ETF held at OCBC contributes to the same economic U.S. exposure as U.S. equities held in the RRSP.
ETF holdings must therefore be looked through when assessing:
geography;
sectors;
individual-company concentration;
factor exposure;
income;
currency exposure.
Account separation does not eliminate economic overlap.
 
12. REQUIRED PROCESS BEFORE ANY DEPLOYMENT RECOMMENDATION
Every portfolio deployment decision must follow this sequence:
1. Retrieve the current portfolio data.
Never rely on historical holdings or memory when current data are available.
2. Identify the account.
Questrade RRSP or OCBC.
3. Identify the exact security.
Confirm ticker, exchange and fund/security identity.
4. Confirm market eligibility.
Ensure the account can purchase the security.
5. Identify trading currency.
6. Check available cash currency.
Determine whether FX conversion is required.
7. Calculate transaction friction.
Include commission and FX considerations.
8. Check executable sizing.
For SGX, enforce 100-share board lots.
9. Examine portfolio overlap and current/target allocation.
10. Determine the security’s portfolio role.
11. Apply the governing KISS/macro framework.
12. Recommend the trade and executable size.
 
13. CRITICAL ERROR-PREVENTION RULE
Never assume a security is currently owned merely because it:
appeared in an older portfolio;
was previously discussed;
appeared on a watchlist;
was considered for purchase;
is contained inside an ETF;
was previously owned;
is held in the other account.
The current portfolio data determine current ownership, position size and target allocation.
Likewise, never infer account location from a ticker or currency alone.
The governing hierarchy is:
CURRENT PORTFOLIO DATA → SECURITY/ETF REFERENCE DATA → ACCOUNT RULES → PORTFOLIO GOVERNANCE → ANALYSIS → EXECUTION
This hierarchy must be followed for every portfolio review, deployment decision and security recommendation.

14. TAX TREATMENT
Questrade RRSP
The RRSP is a Canadian tax-deferred account. Investment income and realized gains can compound without current Canadian taxation while assets remain inside the account. Withdrawals are generally taxable under the applicable Canadian rules.
Tax deferral is an important reason to prioritize long-term total return and compounding rather than current distributable income within this account.
OCBC Brokerage
The OCBC account is a taxable Singapore brokerage account, but Singapore generally does not impose capital gains tax on investment gains and generally does not tax dividends received by individuals from Singapore-resident companies under its one-tier corporate tax system.
For portfolio construction purposes, this makes Singapore-listed dividend equities particularly tax-efficient: dividends from holdings such as DBS, OCBC, Singtel, SGX and ES3 can generally be received without Singapore personal income tax, while capital appreciation is generally not subject to Singapore capital gains tax.
Foreign securities may still be subject to foreign withholding taxes or other source-country taxes, depending on the security’s domicile, structure and underlying income. Therefore, “OCBC = tax free” must not be assumed for every investment held in the account.
Portfolio implication: Singapore dividend stocks can provide relatively tax-efficient spendable income through OCBC, while the RRSP is better viewed as a tax-deferred compounding vehicle. Tax treatment should therefore be considered when deciding which account and security structure best serves a particular portfolio role.
