Glossary Terms
Balance of Payments
One of our Four Horsemen of Economic Risk. Our Balance of Payments scatter plot displays the Current Account Balance as a % of Nominal GDP on the x-axis and the Sovereign Fiscal Balance as a % of Nominal GDP on the y-axis. Geographies clustered near the bottom left have relatively poor balance of payments dynamics and, thus, should generally feature weaker currencies, whereas geographies clustered near the top right have relatively favorable balance of payments dynamics and, thus, should generally feature stronger currencies.
Bottom-Up Macro Regime
The characterization of an economy according to the trending rates of change of growth and inflation. For the purposes of our GRID Regime process, we define the trending rates of change as the trailing 3-month level change of Real GDP YoY and the trailing 3-month level change of Headline CPI YoY. If the trailing 3-month level change of Real GDP YoY is positive and the trailing 3-month level change of Headline CPI YoY is negative, an economy is said to have been in GOLDILOCKS for that month. If the trailing 3-month level change of Real GDP YoY is positive and the trailing 3-month level change of Headline CPI YoY is positive, an economy is said to have been in REFLATION for that month. If the trailing 3-month level change of Real GDP YoY is negative and the trailing 3-month level change of Headline CPI YoY is positive, an economy is said to have been in INFLATION for that month. If the trailing 3-month level change of Real GDP YoY is negative and the trailing 3-month level change of Headline CPI YoY is negative, an economy is said to have been in DEFLATION for that month.
Charm Flows
The need – or lack thereof – of options market makers to delta hedge options they have underwritten to the market due to the passage of time. As options approach maturity, their associated time value decays, causing dealers to unwind any corresponding stock or futures hedges they may have previously established. These hedging flows tend to influence the underlying price of the asset – particularly into OPEX.
Cross-Asset Correction Risk Indicator (CACRI)
CACRI is our primary tool for assessing whether Risk Assets are likely to experience a deep correction or crash over the short-to-medium term. CACRI also helps investors time when it is safe to aggressively buy the dip after a period of heightened cross-asset volatility. CACRI is the sum of the respective Shares of Confirming Markets of INFLATION and DEFLATION – the two "risk off" GRID Regimes in our Top-Down Market Regime nowcasting process. CACRI provides a clean time series-based metric for quantifying the amount of consensus fear or complacency that exists in asset markets, which helps investors quickly and cogently assess the degree to which bearish or bullish positioning is crowded. The data shows investors would do well to book gains in risk assets when CACRI is exiting the Consensus Complacency Zone, insomuch that investors should buy dips in risk assets when CACRI is exiting the Consensus Fear Zone.
Crowding Model
Our Crowding Model is designed to spot extremes in market positioning to tactically fade. CROWDING imbalances are typically resolved in the underlying price of the asset over a short-term time horizon (< 1 month). On the x-axis of our CROWDING Scatter Plots, we display the 14-Day Relative Strength Index ("RSI"). This metric is valuable because it helps investors distinguish which assets are tactically overbought or oversold. On the y-axis of our CROWDING Scatter Plots, we feature the 3mo Z-Score of ETF fund flows as a proxy for capitulatory selling or buying of local lows and highs in the underlying exposure. Bullish signals are derived from the combination of an RSI value below 30 and a minus two sigma reading in ETF fund flows. Bearish signals are derived from the combination of an RSI value above 70 and a plus two sigma reading in ETF fund flows.
Cyclicals/Defensives Ratio
The level of the Russell 1000 Dynamic Index — which tracks the performance of the companies in the Russell 1000 Index with relatively less stable business conditions which are more sensitive to economic cycles, credit cycles, and market cycles — divided by the level of the Russell 1000 Defensives Index — which tracks the performance of the companies in the Russell 1000 Index with relatively more stable business conditions which are less sensitive to economic cycles, credit cycles, and market cycles. This intramarket indicator is valuable for two reasons: 1) because it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-on Market Regime by breaking out to a bullish VAMS condition; and 2) it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-off Market Regime by breaking down to a bearish VAMS condition. These properties are why the Cyclicals/Defensives Ratio is one of our Four Horsemen of Market Risk. The volatility adjustment factor for the Cyclicals/Defensives Ratio VAMS is the Bloomberg US Financial Conditions Index, making the indicator doubly reliable during RORO phase transitions.
Delta Hedging
Delta is the rate of change between the option's price and a $1 change in the underlying asset's price. Due to a confluence of regulatory constraints and firm-level own risk management, options market makers must hedge each option they have underwritten to the market by establishing a corresponding long or short position in the underlying stock or futures markets. Demand for delta hedging is especially high for options with strike prices that are close to the price of the underlying asset and/or are close to maturity.
Demographics
One of our Four Horsemen of Economic Risk. Our Demographics scatter plot displays the 5yr Forward Old-Age Dependency Ratio on the x-axis and the 5yr Forward CAGR of the Working Age Population on the y-axis. Geographies clustered near the top left have high nominal growth potential and, thus, should generally feature higher interest rates, whereas geographies clustered near the bottom right have low nominal growth potential and, thus, should generally feature lower interest rates.
Discretionary Risk Management Overlay aka "Dr. Mo"
The primary tool we use to help our buy side clients and sophisticated retail traders remain on the right side of market risk, which is shorthand for maximizing upside capture in bull markets and minimizing downside capture in bear markets. Dr. Mo is primarily for institutional investors making long-short factor bets (e.g., Growth vs. Value, High Beta vs. Low Beta, US vs. International) to generate relative returns in asset markets. If an ETF is bullish (or bearish) VAMS and that is in line with how the underlying asset should trade in the current Top-Down Market Regime based on historical relative and absolute performance, then Dr. Mo will prescribe a "LONG (or SHORT): Max Position" Proper Trade recommendation. If an ETF is neutral VAMS and the underlying asset should be bullish (or bearish) in the current Market Regime based on historical relative and absolute performance, then Dr. Mo will prescribe a "LONG (or SHORT): Half Position" Proper Trade recommendation. Dr. Mo will prescribe a "No Position" Proper Trade recommendation if the VAMS for the underlying asset is the opposite of what it should be in the current Market Regime based on historical relative and absolute performance. Investors should first determine what a maximum and half position size corresponds to in their portfolio. Then investors should allocate assets according to Dr. Mo's Proper Trade recommendations, only changing positions when the corresponding Proper Trade recommendation changes. Dr. Mo helps clients overcome two of the most challenging behavioral heuristics that prevent investors from achieving their strategic investment objectives – the Action Bias, which describes our tendency to favor action over inaction, and the Illusion of Validity, which describes our tendency to be overconfident in the accuracy of our predictions. Lastly, the color coding of the exposure and its Proper Trade recommendation implies a directionally bullish (light green) or directionally bearish (light red) change in the Proper Trade recommendation from the preceding 42 Macro research report. The color coding of an RSI value means the exposure is tactically oversold (dark green) or tactically overbought (dark red).
Dispersion Model
The spread between the performance of high-performing asset market exposures and their low-performing counterparts. Our Dispersion Model features MoM Sharpe Ratios across ~50 unique US equity sectors and style factors. We use Sharpe Ratios instead of raw performance figures because the volatility adjustment better aligns the analysis with the underlying fund flows into and out of the featured sectors and style factors. This analysis is valuable because changes to the composition of the kinds of sectors and style factors featured in the upper and lower quintiles – specifically whether they are primarily cyclical or defensive in nature – is often a leading indicator for phase transitions to a "risk on" Dominant Market Regime from a "risk off" state prior – and vice versa. In addition to being a leading indicator for our Top-Down Market Regime nowcasting process, there are three ancillary benefits for tracking equity market performance in this matter: 1) it gives us a general sense of what GRID Regime other investors implicitly or explicitly think we are in; 2) it affords us a view on what sectors and style factors multi-manager hedge funds ("Pod Shops") are forcing their PMs into and out of – either implicitly through delta hedging activity or explicitly through capital reallocation; and 3) it affords us a near real-time view on what sectors and style factor exposures Quant funds are bidding up and selling down.
E-Shaped Economy
An E-shaped economy is an economy where high-income households, large corporations, and cash-rich sectors like technology are incrementally achieving prosperity alongside low-to-median-income households, small businesses, and interest-rate-sensitive sectors like housing and consumer durable goods. E-shaped economies are achieved only when fiscal and monetary policy are appropriately calibrated to "grow the economic pie" and distribute wealth morally – two outcomes that many have been deluded into believing cannot coexist due to the loss of Christian values from American politics and the advent of their replacement: economic liberalism, which ranks growing corporate profits above all other human pursuits, including enriching the lives of the very people that create the profits. In aviation terms, an E-shaped economy features the haves and have-nots flying together on a jumbo jet, whereby upper-income cohorts sit up front in first class, middle-income cohorts sit in economy plus, and low-income cohorts sit in the back in basic economy; each cohort takes off, lands – and, occasionally, crashes – together. The 1990s business cycle is a clear example of an E-shaped economy.
Four Horsemen of Economic Risk
A set of macroeconomic indicators that we employ to quantify the secular drivers of asset markets: Demographics, Leverage, Politics, and Balance of Payments. Our Four Horsemen of Economic Risk allow us to readily identify which economies are set up to outperform and underperform on a relative basis across a variety of economic and financial market indicators.
Four Horsemen of Market Risk
A set of intramarket indicators that are at the leading edge of our process for assessing and proactively positioning for changing market conditions because 1) they often break out to a bullish VAMS state ahead of or early in a RORO phase transition to GOLDILOCKS or REFLATION – the two "risk on" Market Regimes in our GRID framework; and 2) they often break down to a bearish VAMS state ahead of or early in a RORO phase transition to INFLATION or DEFLATION – the two "risk off" Market Regimes in our GRID framework: High Beta/Low Beta Ratio, Cyclicals/Defensives Ratio, Small Cap/Mega Cap Ratio, and Value/Growth Ratio.
Gamma Pinning and Unpinning
The rate of change between an option's delta and the underlying asset's price. At-the-money ("ATM") options, near-the-money ("NTM") options, and options that are close to expiry exhibit higher gamma than out-of-the-money ("OTM") options, in-the-money ("ITM") options, and further dated options. Options with high gamma require increasingly outsized delta hedging flows in both directions as the contracts approach maturity. These outsized, back-and-forth delta hedging flows effectively "pin" the price of the underlying asset to the level of the strike with the most open interest, effectively contributing to lower realized volatility into OPEX. Post OPEX, the dissipation of these delta hedging flows effectively "unpins" the price of the underlying asset from the highest gamma strike, leading to higher realized volatility.
Gamma Puke
A self-perpetuating decrease in the underlying price of an asset due to options dealers either being forced to unwind delta hedges associated with having underwritten a surplus of call options to the market ("long gamma") OR being forced to establish new delta hedges associated with having underwritten a surplus of put options to the market ("short gamma").
Gamma Squeeze
A self-perpetuating increase in the underlying price of an asset due to options dealers either being forced to unwind delta hedges associated with having underwritten a surplus of put options to the market ("short gamma") OR being forced to establish new delta hedges associated with having underwritten a surplus of call options to the market ("long gamma").
Global Liquidity Monitor
The primary tool we use to nowcast the key cycles that are most predictive of global liquidity trends and forecast their likely influence upon global liquidity and asset markets. The 42 Macro Global Liquidity Monitor generates forecasts for liquidity by comparing the aggregated correlation-adjusted Z-Score of the YoY percentage change of the leading indicators of global liquidity and calculating the difference between that value and the Z-Score of the YoY percentage change of the 42 Macro Global Liquidity Proxy. A positive residual implies global liquidity is likely to trend higher over the medium term. A negative residual implies global liquidity is likely to trend lower over the medium term. The 42 Macro Global Liquidity Monitor also generates systematic trade ideas for the financial markets of the major economies contributing to the 42 Macro Global Liquidity Proxy. A long (or short) signal in stocks is driven by a rising (or falling) Consensus General Real GDP Expectation with positive (or negative) Economic Surprises. A long (or short) signal in bonds is driven by a falling (or rising) Consensus General Headline CPI Expectation with negative (or positive) Inflation Surprises. A long (or short) signal in currencies is driven by a rising (or falling) Consensus General Nominal GDP Expectation with a positive (or negative) trend in the Policy Rate. The Consensus General Real GDP, Headline CPI, and Nominal GDP Expectations are a blend of current year and next year estimates and reflect the general level of growth and inflation assumed by most investors for each economy.
Global Liquidity Proxy
The 42 Macro Global Liquidity Proxy is equivalent to the aggregated USD sum of major economy central bank balance sheets + the aggregated USD sum of major economy broad money supply + the aggregated USD sum of major economy FX reserves minus gold, divided by the log₁₀ of Bloomberg Global Treasury Bond Aggregate Index volatility to account for the influence of the global repo market on global liquidity. Specifically, rising bond market volatility causes the value and supply of collateral in the repo market to decline, thus perpetuating a decline in repo financing.
Global Macro Risk Matrix
The intermarket analysis model we use to nowcast the Top-Down Market Regime, which is a Bayesian inference process that we repeat daily across 42 of the world's most important macro market indicators spanning a dozen liquid asset classes. Each bullish VAMS reading or bearish VAMS reading in our Global Macro Risk Matrix adds +1pt to each GRID Regime the signal makes sense in from the perspective of how that exposure has historically traded in each regime. For example, a bullish VAMS reading in the S&P 500 would add +1pt to GOLDILOCKS and +1pt to REFLATION, as each is a "risk on" regime in our GRID Regime framework. Conversely, a bearish VAMS reading in the S&P 500 would add +1pt to INFLATION and +1pt to DEFLATION, as each is a "risk off" regime in our GRID Regime framework. Neutral VAMS readings do not contribute to the scores of either GRID Regime.
Global Macro Risk Matrix Backtests
The primary tool we use to communicate the effectiveness of two of our two most powerful signals – the 42 Macro Volatility-Adjusted Momentum Signal (VAMS) and the 42 Macro Global Macro Risk Matrix. The backtests first sum up daily log price change data since January 1st, 1998 for every core asset market exposure and then segments the performance across the various regimes – GOLDILOCKS, REFLATION, INFLATION, DEFLATION, Risk On (G & R), and Risk Off (I & D) – to determine the share of cumulative performance that corresponds to each condition.
GRID Model
The primary tool we use to forecast growth and inflation across geographies and compare our forecasts to Wall Street economist consensus forecasts. We then interpolate our forecasts and consensus estimates into Bottom-Up Macro Regime projections, which allows us to anticipate intervals in which there is a high probability of a RORO phase transition based on a high likelihood of consensus being surprised on the momentum of growth, the momentum of inflation, or both. Investors should use the 42 Macro GRID Model to assess the probability of regime change in asset markets over a medium-to-long-term time horizon.
GRID Regime / Market Regime
Short for GOLDILOCKS, REFLATION, INFLATION, and DEFLATION, GRID Regimes are the backbone of our macro risk management process. The GRID Regimes are valuable because they help investors quickly orient their investment strategy by communicating where and how much to allocate capital within and across asset classes. GOLDILOCKS and REFLATION are both indicative of "risk on" market conditions – the former with a disinflationary bias and the latter with an inflationary bias. INFLATION and DEFLATION are both indicative of "risk off" market conditions – the former with an inflationary bias and the latter with a disinflationary bias.
Growth
The change in the level of economic activity as measured by publicly available and/or widely followed periodic indicators like GDP, Industrial Production, Consumer Spending, PMIs, etc.
High Beta/Low Beta Ratio
The level of the S&P 500 High Beta Index – which tracks the performance of the 100 most volatile stocks in the S&P 500 over the trailing 252 trading days – divided by the level of the S&P 500 Low Volatility Index – which tracks the performance of the 100 least volatile stocks in the S&P 500 over the trailing 252 trading days. This intramarket indicator is valuable for two reasons: 1) because it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-on Market Regime by breaking out to a bullish VAMS condition; and 2) it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-off Market Regime by breaking down to a bearish VAMS condition. These properties are why the High Beta/Low Beta Ratio is among our Four Horsemen of Market Risk. The volatility adjustment factor for the High Beta/Low Beta Ratio VAMS is USD High Yield OAS, making the indicator doubly reliable during RORO phase transitions.
Inflation
The change in the level of prices as measured by publicly available and/or widely followed periodic indicators like the Consumer Price Index, Producer Price Index, Personal Consumption Expenditures Price Index, etc. Our process anchors on Headline CPI because that statistic tends to have the most predictive value with respect to forecasting inflections and/or persistence in the momentum of key financial market indicators like interest rates.
Ivol
The Implied Volatility of a derivative contract on an asset with a specified maturity.

K-Shaped Economy
A K-shaped economy is an economy where high-income households, large corporations, and cash-rich sectors like technology are incrementally achieving prosperity, while low-to-median-income households, small businesses, and interest-rate-sensitive sectors like housing are increasingly falling behind due to limited availability of capital, too-high interest rates, unfair tax policy that overwhelmingly favors capital over labor income, among other things. In aviation terms, a K-shaped economy features the haves flying privately and safely to fancy vacation islands, while the have-nots are flying low-budget airlines to the middle of nowhere… and the planes crash frequently. The post-COVID business cycle is a clear example of a K-shaped economy.

Macro Weather Model
Our Macro Weather Model is designed to accomplish two important tasks. The first is to keep tabs on trends and inflections across the six key macro cycles. The second is to forecast dispersion across asset classes on a short-to-medium-term time horizon. The Macro Weather Model is broken down into the five most important indicators from each cycle. This gives us 30 total macro-market indicators which we then use in our nowcast to provide signals as to what the cycles collectively signal for the stock market, the bond market, the US dollar, gold, commodities, and Bitcoin. Those signals come in three different types: a bull signal, a bear signal, or a neutral signal. These signals can be used to assess what is likely to happen in the next two to three months. To determine whether the growth cycle is a headwind or tailwind for risk assets, the Macro Weather Model tracks impulses in Real GDP ex-Gov't & Net Exports, Real Personal Consumption Expenditures, Private Sector Labor Income, S&P 500 Next Fiscal Year EPS, and our Consensus Short-Run Potential Real GDP Estimate metric, which averages the next two years to smooth out the impact of base effects. A strong positive impulse is when the three-month annualized rate of change exceeds the six-month annualized rate of change, and the six-month annualized rate of change exceeds the year-over-year rate of change. This indicates a trending acceleration in the slope of the underlying time series. The opposite is true for a strong negative impulse. A weak positive impulse is when the three-month annualized rate of change exceeds the six-month annualized rate of change, and the six-month annualized rate of change is less than the year-over-year rate of change. This indicates a transitory acceleration in the slope of the underlying time series. The opposite is true for a weak negative impulse. To determine whether the inflation cycle is a headwind or tailwind for risk assets, the Macro Weather Model tracks impulses in Headline CPI, the Core PCE Price Deflator, CRB Index, 5Y5Y Inflation Swap Rate, and our Consensus Short-Run Potential Headline CPI Estimate, which averages the next two years to smooth out the impact of base effects. To determine whether the monetary policy cycle is a headwind or tailwind for risk assets, the Macro Weather Model tracks impulses in the Fed Funds Rate, 2yr Nominal Treasury Yield - Fed Funds Rate Spread, Fed Treasury Holdings/Marketable Treasury Debt Ratio, Bank Reserves/Commercial Bank Assets Ratio, and Secured Overnight Financing Rate (SOFR) - Interest on Reserves Balances (IORB) Spread. To determine whether the fiscal policy cycle is a headwind or tailwind for risk assets, the Macro Weather Model tracks impulses in the Sovereign Fiscal Balance/GDP Ratio, TTM Federal Revenue, TTM Federal Expenditures, Treasury General Account Balance/Bank Reserves Ratio, and Bills/Marketable Treasury Debt Ratio. To determine whether the liquidity cycle is a headwind or tailwind for risk assets, the Macro Weather Model tracks impulses in the 42 Macro Global Liquidity Proxy, 42 Macro Net Liquidity, MOVE Index, 10yr US Treasury Term Premium, and US Broad Nominal Effective Exchange Rate. We'll unpack the 42 Macro Global Liquidity Proxy and 42 Macro Net Liquidity—which we use to track US liquidity—in the next chapter. Lastly, to determine whether the positioning cycle is a headwind or tailwind for risk assets, the Macro Weather Model tracks levels in the NAAIM Mean Stock Exposure Survey, S&P 500 Price-to-NTM EPS Multiple, S&P 500 3mo Implied Volatility Correlations, US Investment Grade OAS, AAII Bulls-Bears Spread relative to their respective bull market peak and trough thresholds.

Paradigm A
The 2020–21 lurching into fiscal dominance which helped catalyze the geopolitically driven supply-demand imbalance in the US Treasury bond market that is likely to persist – and widen – throughout the duration of this Fourth Turning.
Paradigm B
History confirms that when sovereigns get too indebted – specifically when both the stock and flow of sovereign debt supply increasingly exceed available resources to capitalize the leverage – there are only three options to remedy the issue. Paradigm B is the cut phase of the cut → grow → print sequence required to address the geopolitically driven supply-demand imbalance in the US Treasury bond market. Cutting requires a reduction in fiscal expenditures and a reduction in trade deficits; both outcomes increase net national savings.
Paradigm C
History confirms that when sovereigns get too indebted – specifically when both the stock and flow of sovereign debt supply increasingly exceed available resources to capitalize the leverage – there are only three options to remedy the issue. Paradigm C is the grow phase of the cut → grow → print sequence required to address the geopolitically driven supply-demand imbalance in the US Treasury bond market. Growing requires the adoption of a growth-friendly policy mix featuring tax cuts, deregulation, and credit easing. The goal of the grow phase is to delever the public sector balance sheet by increasing the denominator (e.g., GDP, GDI, domestic liquidity) faster than the numerator (i.e., sovereign debt).
Paradigm D
History confirms that when sovereigns get too indebted – specifically when both the stock and flow of sovereign debt supply increasingly exceed available resources to capitalize the leverage – there are only three options to remedy the issue. Paradigm D is the print phase of the cut → grow → print sequence required to address the geopolitically driven supply-demand imbalance in the US Treasury bond market. Printing requires an erosion of central bank independence that results in the monetary authority monetizing debt – either explicitly via quantitative easing (QE) and/or yield curve control (YCC), or implicitly via reserve management operations (RMOP). The goal of the print phase is to lessen the impact of crowding out by the bloated public sector balance sheet upon the private sector. The Treasury market is atop the global capital structure, so until the US dollar is no longer the dominant reserve currency, it will always attract the capital it needs at the expense of the private sector – particularly low-to-median-income households, small businesses, and interest-rate-sensitive sectors like housing and consumer durable goods.

Positioning Model
The 42 Macro Positioning Model identifies extreme values in time series that have been proven across market cycles to closely track investor positioning, with the express intent of signaling a high probability of a pending correction within a bull market, a crash from a bull market, a sharp bounce within a bear market, or a sustained recovery from a bear market. Each indicator is displayed on a percentile basis relative to its historical time series. Extreme values are the median observations for each respective time series observed at S&P 500 bull market peaks and S&P 500 bear market troughs. Probable Range Model
The primary tool we use to spot inflection points in the short-term momentum of a security or index. Our Probable Range Model offers a thoughtful evolution upon J. Welles Wilder's Average True Range framework by explicitly linking the calculus of the Upper and Lower Boundaries of the Probable Range to a given exposure's VAMS state, whereby the range features a positive skew if the exposure is bullish VAMS, a Gaussian skew if the exposure is neutral VAMS, and a negative skew if the exposure is bearish VAMS. This regime-switching feature is especially valuable because it helps investors reorient their risk management approach – i.e. buying dips vs. selling rips – much faster than traditional trading range processes during phase transitions in the medium-term price momentum and volatility characteristics of the asset.
RORO Phase Transition
An inflection in the Market Regime from a risk-on condition (i.e., GOLDILOCKS or REFLATION) to a risk-off condition (i.e., INFLATION or DEFLATION) – or vice versa. Empirically, RORO phase transitions have occurred slightly less than three times per year, across multiple lookback periods (e.g., trailing 5, 10, 15, and 20 years). That means active investors have historically only needed to make three broad risk management pivots per year to remain on the right side of market risk – which itself is shorthand for maximizing upside capture in bull markets and minimizing downside capture in bear markets.
Rvol
The Realized Volatility of an asset over a specified duration.
Sectors
The grouping of stocks and credits according to how the businesses make money, which itself is a function of the products they sell and the customers they serve.
Share of Confirming Markets
Each individual GRID Regime's Sum of Confirming Markets divided by the total Sum of Confirming Markets across all four GRID Regimes at each daily interval. This metric is valuable because it allows investors to quantify the relative influence of each GRID Regime upon the evolution of market narratives, implied changes in investor positioning, and, most importantly, the relative and absolute performance of asset markets themselves.

Small Cap/Mega Cap Ratio
The level of the Russell 2000 Index – which tracks the performance of the 2000 smallest stocks by market cap in the Russell 3000 – divided by the level of the S&P 100 – which tracks the performance of the 100 largest stocks by market cap in the S&P 500. This intramarket indicator is valuable for two reasons: 1) because it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-on Market Regime by breaking out to a bullish VAMS condition; and 2) it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-off Market Regime by breaking down to a bearish VAMS condition. These properties are why the Small Cap/Mega Cap Ratio is among our Four Horsemen of Market Risk. The volatility adjustment factor for the Small Cap/Mega Cap Ratio VAMS is the S&P 500 3mo Implied Correlation Index, making the indicator doubly reliable during RORO phase transitions.
Strength of Signal
The Strength of Signal is the cumulative Sum of Confirming Markets across the four GRID Regimes, with the values displayed on a percentile basis since the start of 1998. The metric is valuable because it allows investors to determine how strong of a "risk on" or "risk off" signal investors are receiving from the key macro market indicators featured in our Global Macro Risk Matrix. We consider readings in the lower quintile to be depressed and indicative of a high degree of uncertainty being priced into asset markets. We apply a reflexivity framework to further interpret that implied uncertainty as suggestive of consensus positioning for the highest scoring GRID Regime(s) as being depressed from the perspectives of leverage and crowding. We consider readings in the upper quintile to be elevated and indicative of a high degree of certainty being priced into asset markets. We apply a reflexivity framework to further interpret that implied certainty as suggestive of consensus positioning for the highest scoring GRID Regime(s) as being elevated from the perspectives of leverage and crowding.
Strong Negative Impulse
A strong negative impulse is when the three-month annualized rate of change is less than the six-month annualized rate of change, and the six-month annualized rate of change is less than the year-over-year rate of change. This indicates a trending deceleration in the slope of the underlying time series.
Strong Positive Impulse
A strong positive impulse is when the three-month annualized rate of change is greater than the six-month annualized rate of change, and the six-month annualized rate of change is greater than the year-over-year rate of change. This indicates a trending acceleration in the slope of the underlying time series.
Style Factors
The grouping of stocks and credits according to the characteristics of securities themselves, which are usually in relation to a larger universe. For example, High Beta stocks are stocks that have a beta that exceeds the mean or median beta of the broader market, insomuch that High Yield stocks are stocks that have a dividend or free cash flow yield that exceeds the mean or median dividend or free cash flow yield of the broader market.
Sum of Confirming Markets
The number of macro market indicators featured in our Global Macro Risk Matrix that are confirming of each GRID Regime at each daily interval. This metric is valuable because it allows investors to quantify the relative influence of each GRID Regime upon the evolution of market narratives, changes in investor behavior, and, most importantly, the relative and absolute performance of asset markets themselves.
Top-Down Market Regime
The GRID Regime with the highest Share of Confirming Markets is said to be the Top-Down Market Regime. The Top-Down Market Regime is very important to our investment process because it dictates the asset allocation (e.g., how many stocks vs. how many bonds) and portfolio construction (e.g., what type of stocks and what type of bonds) recommendations we communicate to clients. At all times, investors are likely to experience the best performance by positioning their portfolios – or at least any incremental bets – in alignment with the Top-Down Market Regime. Responding to phase transitions in the Top-Down Market Regime early and with conviction is the key to remaining on the right side of market risk, which is shorthand for maximizing upside capture in bull markets and minimizing downside capture in bear markets. We feature a collection of high-quality quantitative and qualitative signals throughout our macro risk management process to help clients build or erode conviction in the sustainability of the current Market Regime and develop conviction in what the next Market Regime might be. Understanding the likely sequence and timing of Market Regime phase transitions ex ante is helpful to clients that prefer low portfolio turnover so they can allocate assets appropriately. Understanding the likely sequence and timing of Market Regime phase transitions ex ante is not necessary, however. The 42 Macro Global Macro Risk Matrix Backtests prove just how effective it is to dispassionately respect the evolution of the Market Regime irrespective of one's fundamental views. Said simply, the 42 Macro Top-Down Market Regime nowcasting process proves investors do not have to predict the future to make and save lots of money in financial markets.
Type 1 vs. Type 2 Errors
The most common investment mistakes are Type 1 and Type 2 errors. A Type 1 error is a "false positive," where a decision to buy (or sell) is not rewarded by subsequent appreciation (or depreciation) of the asset. Conversely, a Type 2 error is a "false negative," where a decision not to buy (or not to sell) is punished by subsequent appreciation (or depreciation) of the asset. Type 1 errors are the result of investors attempting to remain on the right side of developing market risk by positioning according to evolving market dynamics—e.g., longing "expensive" stocks that have positive momentum. Type 2 errors are the result of investors attempting to predict a reversal of developing market risk by taking the other side of evolving market dynamics—such as shorting "expensive" stocks that have positive momentum. Type 1 errors result from markets that fail to trend and, because markets trend most of the time (~75% for the S&P 500 and ~90% for Bitcoin), Type 1 errors tend to accumulate relatively small losses. Type 2 errors result from markets that fail to reverse course and tend to accumulate much more hazardous large losses. Type 2 errors occur when investors erroneously think they are smarter than the market. In short, Type 1 errors protect investors from experiencing FOMO—the fear of missing out, which causes investors to buy cycle tops—and FOML—the fear of more losses, which causes investors to sell cycle lows. Conversely, Type 2 errors inevitably result in FOMO and FOML. A disciplined process that adapts as conditions evolve allows investors to participate in long-term upside while mitigating downside risk. The combination of the two allows the net asset value of investors' portfolios to grow faster because the exponential growth from compounding returns builds wealth faster with greater quantities than it does with smaller quantities. By design, the 42 Macro framework accepts uncertainty and focuses on responding systematically to observable changes in the market regime, momentum, and liquidity rather than constantly trying to predict all these difficult-to-forecast dynamics or, worse, reacting emotionally to them.
Value/Growth Ratio
The level of the Russell 1000 Value Index – which tracks the performance of the 1000 largest US equities by market cap that also feature below-median price-to-book ratios and forecasted growth rates – divided by the level of the Russell 1000 Growth Index – which tracks the performance of the 1000 largest US equities by market cap that also feature above-median price-to-book ratios and forecasted growth rates. This intramarket indicator is valuable for two reasons: 1) because it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-on Market Regime by breaking out to a bullish VAMS condition; and 2) it can be a leading indicator of a RORO phase transition to a risk-off Market Regime by breaking down to a bearish VAMS condition. These properties are why the Value/Growth Ratio is among our Four Horsemen of Market Risk. The volatility adjustment factor for the Value/Growth Ratio VAMS is the US Dollar Index, making the indicator doubly reliable during RORO phase transitions.
VAMS Backtests
The primary tool we use to communicate the effectiveness of two of our two most powerful signals – the 42 Macro Volatility-Adjusted Momentum Signal (VAMS) and the 42 Macro Global Macro Risk Matrix. The backtests first sum up daily log price change data since January 1st, 1998 for every core asset market exposure and then segments the performance across the various regimes – bullish, neutral, and bearish – to determine the share of cumulative performance that corresponds to each condition.
Vanna Flows
The need – or lack thereof – of options market makers to delta hedge options they have underwritten to the market due to changes in implied volatility. As Ivol rises / falls, the price of the option rises / falls, causing dealers to increase / decrease the notional value of any corresponding stock or futures hedges they may have previously established. Volatility-Adjusted Momentum Signal (VAMS) Model
The primary tool we use to signal trends and inflections in momentum in asset markets. The volatility adjustment is valuable because it tends to be a leading indicator for breakouts or breakdowns in medium-term price momentum. The model itself is a dynamic factor model based on a cumulative scoring system whereby an asset receives -2pts (bearish VAMS), 0pts (neutral VAMS), +2pts (bullish VAMS) via a -1pt/+1pt contribution if the short-term moving average of price is below/above its medium-term moving average and a -1pt/+1pt contribution if the short-term moving average of volatility is above/below the median of the corresponding medium-term sample – or vice versa, depending on the asset's historical correlation to its implied or realized volatility. For example, interest rates, cryptocurrencies, and implied volatility tend to be positively correlated with their respective volatilities, whereas equities, credit, and commodities tend to be inversely correlated with their respective volatilities.
Weak Negative Impulse
A weak negative impulse is when the three-month annualized rate of change is less than the six-month annualized rate of change, and the six-month annualized rate of change is greater than the year-over-year rate of change. This indicates a transitory deceleration in the slope of the underlying time series.
Weak Positive Impulse
A weak positive impulse is when the three-month annualized rate of change is greater than the six-month annualized rate of change, and the six-month annualized rate of change is less than the year-over-year rate of change. This indicates a transitory acceleration in the slope of the underlying time series.
Z-score
Z-score is a simple statistical measurement that describes how far a specific value is from the mean (average) of a dataset, expressed in terms of standard deviations. A Z-score of 0 means the value is exactly at the mean, while a positive Z-score means the value is above the mean and a negative Z-score means the value is below the mean.
