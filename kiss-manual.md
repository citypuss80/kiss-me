KISS UNDERSTANDING MANUAL
1. Purpose of This Manual
This manual defines how 42 Macro’s framework is to be understood and applied within this portfolio project.
Its primary purpose is to prevent different parts of the 42 Macro system from being conflated. KISS, the Global Macro Risk Matrix, VAMS, Dr. Mo, GRID, forward-looking quantitative models and fundamental macro research answer different questions across different time horizons.
The central hierarchy is:
KISS = how much portfolio risk we take.
Dr. Mo = where tactical and relative opportunities exist within markets.
Forward quantitative models = what may happen next.
Fundamental research = why it may happen.
For this portfolio, KISS is the master asset-allocation signal. Macro forecasts, valuation opinions, market narratives and discretionary judgment do not independently override KISS.
2. The Four Market Regimes
42 Macro classifies market regimes according to the direction of growth and inflation:
Regime	Growth	Inflation	Character
GOLDILOCKS	Rising	Falling	Risk-on / disinflationary
REFLATION	Rising	Rising	Risk-on / inflationary
INFLATION	Falling	Rising	Risk-off / inflationary
DEFLATION	Falling	Falling	Risk-off / disinflationary

The fundamental distinction is:
GOLDILOCKS + REFLATION = Risk-On
INFLATION + DEFLATION = Risk-Off
The inflation direction differentiates the two regimes within each risk state.
 
3. Global Macro Risk Matrix
The Global Macro Risk Matrix identifies the market regime that financial markets are currently pricing.
It is a market-regime nowcast, not simply a discretionary economic forecast.
42 Macro evaluates market indicators using volatility-adjusted momentum and determines how consistent their behavior is with the four macro regimes. The regime receiving the greatest confirmation becomes the prevailing Top-Down Market Regime.
This distinction is critical:
The Global Macro Risk Matrix tells us what regime markets are pricing NOW.
It does not necessarily tell us which economic regime 42 Macro expects several months from now.
That is the job of the forward-looking models.
 
4. KISS — Keep It Simple & Systematic
KISS is 42 Macro’s systematic, long-only risk-management strategy designed for long-term, relatively low-turnover investors.
Its default strategic portfolio is:
60% Stocks
30% Gold
10% Bitcoin
with Cash becoming the residual allocation whenever the risk-management signals reduce those exposures.
The default instruments are:
Stocks — VT
Gold — GLDM
Bitcoin — FBTC
Cash — USFR
The instruments and strategic weights can be customized. The important part of KISS is the risk-management process and signals, not the specific ETFs used in the default portfolio.
KISS operates in two stages:
Market Regime → Top-Down Target Allocation → VAMS → Actual Exposure
 
5. KISS Top-Down Overlay
The prevailing Market Regime determines the maximum target allocation permitted for each asset class.
Stocks
GOLDILOCKS / REFLATION: 60% target
INFLATION / DEFLATION: 30% target
Gold
GOLDILOCKS / REFLATION / DEFLATION: 30% target
INFLATION: 15% target
Bitcoin
GOLDILOCKS / REFLATION: 10% target
INFLATION / DEFLATION: 5% target
These are Top-Down Target Allocations.
They are not necessarily the exposures actually held.
Actual exposure is determined by VAMS.
 
6. VAMS — Volatility-Adjusted Momentum Signal
VAMS is 42 Macro’s Volatility-Adjusted Momentum Signal.
It evaluates the direction and strength of an asset’s price trend while accounting for volatility.
VAMS can be:
Bullish
Neutral
Bearish
Within KISS:
VAMS	Position
Bullish	100% of Top-Down Target
Neutral	50% of Top-Down Target
Bearish	0% of Top-Down Target

Therefore:
Actual Exposure = Top-Down Target × VAMS Position Size
Example:
If the Market Regime is REFLATION, Stocks receive a 60% Top-Down Target.
If Stock VAMS is:
Bullish → 60% Stocks
Neutral → 30% Stocks
Bearish → 0% Stocks
The unused allocation becomes cash.
 
7. Why KISS Can Hold Cash During a Risk-On Regime
A Risk-On regime does not automatically mean KISS holds the full strategic portfolio.
The Market Regime determines the maximum target.
VAMS determines whether that target is actually deployed.
For example:
REFLATION + Bullish Stock VAMS = 60% Stocks
but:
REFLATION + Bearish Gold VAMS = 0% Gold
and:
REFLATION + Bearish Bitcoin VAMS = 0% Bitcoin
The resulting portfolio would therefore be:
60% Stocks / 0% Gold / 0% Bitcoin / 40% Cash
This is not contradictory.
The correct mental model is:
Regime determines how much exposure is permitted. VAMS determines how much exposure is actually held.
 
8. KISS Is the Master Allocation Signal
For this portfolio project, KISS determines whether portfolio-level equity risk deserves capital.
Macro analysis may identify:
recession risk;
sticky inflation;
deteriorating liquidity;
Treasury-market instability;
excessive positioning;
weakening labor markets;
geopolitical risks;
valuation concerns;
or another potential future problem.
None of these independently constitutes a portfolio-level KISS sell signal.
The portfolio does not attempt to anticipate every regime transition.
The objective is to respond sufficiently quickly after the systematic evidence confirms that conditions have changed.
This is the “fast follower” principle.
Missing the exact market top or bottom is acceptable. Avoiding the bulk of a major adverse regime is more important than perfect timing.
 
9. GRID
GRID is an economic-regime framework.
Like the Market Regime system, it evaluates combinations of growth and inflation:
Goldilocks — Growth ↑ / Inflation ↓
Reflation — Growth ↑ / Inflation ↑
Inflation — Growth ↓ / Inflation ↑
Deflation — Growth ↓ / Inflation ↓
However:
GRID and the Global Macro Risk Matrix are not interchangeable.
GRID helps assess the likely economic regime over a forward horizon.
The Global Macro Risk Matrix identifies the current regime being priced by markets.
Therefore:
Market Regime = REFLATION
GRID modal outcome = GOLDILOCKS
can both be true simultaneously.
This is not a contradiction and must never be “corrected” by forcing the two models to agree.
 
10. Forward-Looking Quantitative Models
42 Macro uses several quantitative tools to evaluate how the macro environment may evolve.
These include:
Macro Weather Model
GRID Model
Global Liquidity Model
Positioning Model
These models generally operate over approximately a 3–12 month forward horizon.
Their purpose is to help identify conditions that could eventually produce a Market Regime change.
They are early-warning and probability-distribution tools.
They are not substitutes for KISS.
 
11. Macro Weather
Macro Weather evaluates important macro cycles such as:
Growth
Inflation
Monetary Policy
Fiscal Policy
Liquidity
Positioning
Its purpose is to determine whether these forces are becoming more or less supportive of future economic and market outcomes.
A deterioration in Macro Weather should therefore be interpreted as:
The probability of a less favorable future regime may be increasing.
It should not automatically be interpreted as:
Sell equities now.
KISS remains the allocation authority.
 
12. Global Liquidity
42 Macro evaluates private- and public-sector liquidity conditions and leading indicators of changes in global liquidity.
Liquidity conditions can materially influence financial asset performance.
Conceptually:
Improving liquidity → generally supportive of risk assets
Deteriorating liquidity → generally less supportive of risk assets
However, liquidity is a forward-looking risk input, not an independent KISS allocation signal.
Deteriorating liquidity may increase the probability of a future regime transition without requiring immediate portfolio de-risking.
 
13. Positioning and Crowding
42 Macro evaluates investor positioning and market crowding to identify conditions in which markets may be vulnerable to significant reversals.
These tools can indicate:
excessive bullish positioning;
excessive bearish positioning;
elevated correction risk;
elevated crash risk;
potential short squeezes;
or tactical opportunities created by extreme positioning.
Positioning and crowding operate over shorter horizons than the structural Macro Narrative.
A crowded market does not automatically mean the underlying fundamental thesis is wrong.
It means the probability or magnitude of a tactical reversal may be elevated.
 
14. Probable Range
Probable Range analysis helps determine whether an asset is statistically extended relative to its expected trading range.
Typical descriptions include:
Overbought
or
Oversold
These are tactical conditions.
They must not be confused with VAMS or Market Regime.
For example, an asset can simultaneously be:
Bullish VAMS + Overbought
This means the trend remains bullish while price has become tactically extended.
Neither observation invalidates the other.
 
15. Dr. Mo — Discretionary Risk Management Overlay
Dr. Mo is 42 Macro’s Discretionary Risk Management Overlay, intended to provide more granular tactical signals across global markets.
Despite “Discretionary” appearing in its name, the underlying Proper Trade signals are systematically generated.
Dr. Mo combines two primary inputs:
1. Top-Down Market Regime
What an asset historically should be doing in the prevailing regime.
2. Bottom-Up VAMS
What the asset’s volatility-adjusted momentum is actually doing.
The combination determines the Proper Trade.
Conceptually:
Market Regime determines preferred direction → VAMS confirms or rejects the trade and determines position size → Dr. Mo produces the Proper Trade signal.
 
16. Dr. Mo Proper Trade Signals
Typical Dr. Mo states include:
Proper Trade	Interpretation
LONG: Max Position	Regime and bullish VAMS align
LONG: Half Position	Regime favors long; VAMS is neutral
SHORT: Max Position	Regime and bearish VAMS align
SHORT: Half Position	Regime favors short; VAMS is neutral, where permitted
No Position	VAMS conflicts with expected regime behavior

Dr. Mo therefore answers a different question from KISS.
KISS asks:
How much strategic portfolio risk should be deployed?
Dr. Mo asks:
Which assets, factors, sectors or markets currently offer systematically supported tactical exposures?

18. KISS and Dr. Mo Are Independent
This is a critical rule.
KISS and Dr. Mo are independent risk-management processes.
A Dr. Mo Proper Trade signal must not automatically be interpreted as a change to KISS.
For example, if Dr. Mo generates a bearish or short signal for an asset while KISS continues to allocate to that asset class, this does not mean the two systems need to be reconciled.
They are answering different portfolio-construction questions.
Therefore:
Never use a Dr. Mo Proper Trade signal as though it were a KISS allocation signal.
For this portfolio:
KISS = portfolio-level asset-allocation authority
Dr. Mo = secondary tactical and portfolio-construction evidence
 
19. How Dr. Mo Is Used in This Portfolio
Once KISS has determined the amount of portfolio risk permitted, Dr. Mo may help determine where that permitted risk is most attractively deployed.
For example, if KISS permits full equity exposure and Dr. Mo indicates systematic support for:
International > US
Value > Growth
Cyclicals > Defensives
those observations can inform security selection and deployment within the equity allocation.
They do not increase or decrease the amount of equity exposure authorized by KISS.
The hierarchy is therefore:
Constitution → KISS → Portfolio Construction / Dr. Mo → Execution

The broad framework includes:
Paradigm A — Treasury supply/demand imbalance
The underlying structural problem.

23. How to Interpret Conflicting Signals
Different parts of the 42 Macro system can legitimately disagree because they operate over different horizons.
For example:
KISS: Risk-On
VAMS: Bullish Stocks
GRID: Future Goldilocks
Liquidity: Deteriorating
Positioning: Crowded
Fundamental Research: Increasing inflation risk
These observations are not necessarily contradictory.
The correct interpretation could be:
Markets remain in a confirmed risk-on regime and KISS therefore maintains equity exposure. Forward-looking liquidity and positioning indicate increasing vulnerability, while fundamental research identifies inflation as an emerging risk. These raise the probability of a future regime change but do not constitute a current KISS sell signal.
Do not attempt to make every model tell the same story.
 
24. Fast-Follower Philosophy
The portfolio does not attempt to predict the exact moment a bull market ends or a bear market begins.
KISS deliberately waits for systematic confirmation.
This creates the possibility of:
selling after a decline has already begun;
buying after a recovery has already started;
false defensive signals;
temporary whipsaws;
and missing portions of market advances.
These are accepted costs of the system.
The objective is not perfect timing.
The objective is to participate in the bulk of favorable regimes while avoiding the bulk of prolonged adverse regimes.
 
25. Type I vs Type II Errors
The framework accepts the possibility of false alarms in order to reduce the risk of failing to respond to a genuine major regime change.
A false defensive signal can create an opportunity cost.
Failing to respond to a major bear-market transition can create permanent or prolonged capital impairment.
For this portfolio, the second error is considered substantially more damaging.
Therefore, the portfolio accepts imperfect signals and occasional whipsaws as part of systematic drawdown management.
 
26. Rules for Analyzing New 42 Macro Material
Whenever new 42 Macro material is supplied, analyze it in the following order:
1. KISS
Has the actual recommended Stocks, Gold, Bitcoin or Cash allocation changed?
2. Global Macro Risk Matrix
Has the prevailing Market Regime changed?
3. VAMS
Have relevant asset VAMS signals changed?
4. Dr. Mo
Have relevant Proper Trade, sector, factor, geographic or asset signals changed?
5. Forward-Looking Models
Have GRID, Macro Weather, Liquidity, Positioning, Crowding or other quantitative indicators materially changed?
6. Fundamental Macro Narrative
Does the new information strengthen, weaken, invalidate or create any structural macro themes?
These categories must remain separate in the analysis.
 
27. Hard Interpretation Rules
The following rules must always be observed:
KISS is the master portfolio-level asset-allocation signal.
Do not override KISS using discretionary macro forecasts.
Do not use GRID as though it were the current Market Regime.
Do not use a Dr. Mo Proper Trade signal as though it were a KISS signal.
Do not treat positioning, crowding or probable-range conditions as thesis invalidation.
Do not interpret a forward-looking warning as proof that a regime change has already occurred.
Do not force conflicting signals from different horizons into agreement.
Do not assume a 42 Macro forecast is a portfolio instruction unless the relevant risk-management system actually produces an actionable signal.
Do not confuse the default 60/30/10 KISS portfolio with this portfolio’s customized implementation.
Do not allow individual security opinions, valuations, forecasts, news or emotions to override the portfolio’s governing allocation process.
 
28. Portfolio Decision Hierarchy
For this portfolio, the hierarchy is:
1. Portfolio Constitution
2. KISS — portfolio-level risk allocation
3. Portfolio construction — what deserves capital
4. Dr. Mo and other relevant tactical evidence — where/how permitted capital may be deployed
5. Execution rules — when/how positions are entered or adjusted
The Macro Narrative informs every level by improving understanding of the environment.
It does not supersede the hierarchy.
 
29. Master Interpretation
When reviewing 42 Macro material, always keep the following distinction in mind:
KISS tells us what to do now.
Dr. Mo tells us where tactical and relative opportunities exist.
Forward-looking quantitative models tell us what may happen next.
Fundamental macro research tells us why it may happen.
A deteriorating forecast is a warning.
A changing macro thesis is information.
An extreme positioning signal is tactical evidence.
A confirmed KISS change is an asset-allocation instruction.
These must never be treated as equivalent.
